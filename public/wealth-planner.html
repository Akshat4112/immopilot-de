How to interpret the long-term results

What this calculator does and does not include

Included: buy-your-own-home or rental-investment-only strategy with existing mortgaged, mortgage-free or rented housing; rental cash-flow bridge and asset-composition chart; up to four independent German rental properties and 2026 incremental rental-income tax estimates; individual or jointly assessed salaries, German payroll estimates, GKV/PKV, living expenses, cash reserve projection, spouse-specific investments, optional monthly contributions, mortgage amortization, extra annual repayments, purchase costs, monthly owner costs, fixed-rate expiry and hypothetical refinancing, ETF fees, 2026 German individual/joint capital-income tax allowance, return scenarios, share-sale requests, house-price growth and inflation-adjusted home-equity illustration.

Investment taxation: a qualifying equity ETF has a 30% partial exemption. The scenario tax assumes entire positive annual gains are realized. The withdrawal projection uses proportional average cost basis (rather than FIFO), with the appropriate individual/joint allowance; separate spousal assessments use an approximate evenly split unused allowance. Actual German sales are generally FIFO and can differ.

Not modeled accurately: special rental depreciation (§7b etc.), differentiated tax treatment of furnished rental, non-full-year acquisitions, changed rental intention, extraordinary Sonderumlagen, financed renovations, recapture or clawback tax cases, precise rental tax return; Vorabpauschale cash tax and later offset, annual tax reforms, acquisition-specific cost basis, full employee payroll reconciliation and exact spouse-specific tax allocations, broker spreads, investment-market volatility sequences beyond the selected initial crash, mortgage refinancing fees, and differences in actual house-selling costs.

Risk: 7% return does not mean 7% distributable income. Never make a mortgage dependent on an ETF return assumption. Maintain an emergency cash reserve and plan from salary affordability.

References: InvStG §20 (fund exemptions), EStG §20 (capital-income allowances), EStG §32d (capital-income taxation). Illustrative planning tool, not financial or tax advice. German finance glossary and calculation notes

AfA — building depreciation

Depreciation can reduce taxable rental income, but not own-use income. The applicable rate and building share depend on property facts and dates.

Werbungskosten — rental expenses

Eligible rental-related expenses may include interest, management and qualifying repairs. Mortgage principal is not deductible.

Hausgeld and maintenance reserve

Some owner charges can be passed to tenants. Contributions to a WEG maintenance reserve are generally not immediately tax-deductible merely upon payment.

Sondertilgung — extra mortgage repayment

An optional additional principal payment, subject to mortgage terms, can reduce future interest costs.

Vorabpauschale — ETF advance taxation

German fund taxation can create a taxable amount even without a sale; the calculator provides only approximate ETF tax estimates.

Cash flow vs investment returns

Unrealized ETF growth is not spendable cash. Tax benefits may arise only at assessment or through adjusted withholding.

Planning tool, not individual tax or investment advice. Recheck current German tax rules and lender terms.
