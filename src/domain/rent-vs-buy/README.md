# Rent versus buy (CF-009)

`calculateRentVersusBuy` consumes the available CF-003 financing result and the **actual CF-005/006 amortization schedule**, without constructing another schedule. It implements PD-007 §9's owner-occupier matched-budget comparison. The PD-010 owner-occupier scenario provides cent-exact endpoint and break-even vectors. Rental-investment scenarios belong to a different calculator.

## Monthly accounting

- Renter starts with the buyer's required equity invested; buyer starts with no alternative portfolio. The buyer owns the property and owes the mortgage debt.
- Each household faces the same monthly budget: the greater of buyer and renter housing cash outflows. The cheaper option invests the difference at month-end, after its existing portfolio earns the monthly effective return. Principal repayment is **not** deducted again as a wealth expense: the real mortgage balance falls in the supplied schedule.
- Buyer cash outflow is the schedule's **actual regular payment**, including its final-payment adjustment, plus projected monthly owner costs. After payoff, regular payment and debt are zero. Rent and owner costs use their monthly effective growth factors, with each month's cash amount independently half-up rounded to cents.
- If the supplied schedule contains Sondertilgung within the comparison horizon, `includeAdditionalRepaymentsInMatchedBudget` **must** be true; that actual additional cash outflow enters the common budget and the renter invests the corresponding difference. If false, the comparison returns `UNMATCHED_ADDITIONAL_REPAYMENTS` instead of claiming two households have equal resources. The choice must be explicit even when no extra repayment is present.
- Buyer liquidation net wealth = projected property value − optional hypothetical selling costs − actual closing mortgage debt + buyer alternative portfolio. Renter net wealth = renter portfolio. Selling costs default to zero if omitted; any nonzero rate is hypothetical, applied to property value at each month, and does not modify the amortization. Break-even is the first **month ≥1** with unrounded buyer-minus-renter wealth ≥0 within the specified horizon, or the distinct `not-reached-within-horizon` result. The reported year is `ceil(month / 12)`.

Annual property appreciation, rent growth, owner-cost growth and alternative portfolio return are effective annual decimal rates strictly greater than −1; monthly factor is `(1 + annualRate)^(1/12)`. Portfolio and property are kept at high precision; only reported money is independently half-up rounded to integer cents. Mortgage balances and scheduled cash are already integer cents. Difference and both net wealth figures are independently rounded, so the reported difference need not equal the subtraction of the two independently rounded displayed figures by one cent.

The period after contractual **Zinsbindung** uses the original supplied constant-rate schedule as a **projection only**, not a guaranteed refinancing term. `mortgageProjectionAssumption` labels this explicitly. An unavailable financing result, amortization result, cash gap, principal mismatch, unpaired Sondertilgung, or missing row before payoff yields a typed unavailable result; validation errors retain stable codes and field names. Analysis horizon is 1–1,200 whole months, and the comparison cannot extrapolate a missing debt balance.

## Exclusions and limits

Educational pre-tax estimates, not financial, tax or legal advice. No rental-income or capital-gains taxation, mortgage interest tax effects, sale taxes, rent-control rules, landlord restrictions, transaction costs on renter investments, broker fees on resale beyond the optional selling-cost percentage, refinancing fees or future-rate forecast. Initial acquisition costs and renovation/setup are represented by CF-003 required equity, not charged a second time. Monthly owner costs must be a realistic editable total (e.g. maintenance, service charges, non-recoverable costs); omitted costs, vacancies, major repairs, inflation, investment risk, property price uncertainty, and timing of move/sale can change the outcome. A constant annual growth or return assumption is illustrative rather than a guarantee. The equal-budget model does not measure household utility or mortgage eligibility.
